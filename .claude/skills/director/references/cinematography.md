# Cinematography & Editing Grammar

The vocabulary you choose from when directing, and the reasons behind each
choice. Every decision should be traceable to an emotional intent or to the
style profile, never to "it looks cool".

## 1. Intent → craft map

Starting points, not laws. The profile overrides them.

| Intent | Size | Angle | Lens | Movement | Light | Cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intimacy / confession | CU–ECU | eye level | 85–135, shallow | slow push-in or locked | soft key, low ratio, warm | long holds |
| Isolation / loneliness | EWS, subject small | slightly high | wide or very long | static or slow pull-out | cool, negative space | slow |
| Power / heroic reveal | low WS–MS | low | 24–35 | crane up, orbit, push | rim + high contrast, backlight | hold on reveal, then cut on action |
| Unease / paranoia | MCU, short-sided | Dutch, high | wide close to face | handheld creep, slow zoom | hard top light, deep shadows | irregular, jump cuts |
| Energy / hype | mix, many inserts | varied | wide + long mix | whip pans, snap zooms, handheld | saturated, practical colour | on the beat, 0.3–1 s |
| Awe / scale | EWS → WS | low or aerial | 14–24 | drone push, slow crane, time-lapse | golden hour, volumetric haze | long, music-led |
| Melancholy | profile MS | eye level | 50–85 | slow pull-out, drift | blue hour, desaturated | lingering, dissolves |
| Tension → release | CU tightening, then WS | eye level | lengthening | push-ins accelerating, then release | narrowing pool of light | cuts accelerating, then a long hold |
| Comedy (deadpan) | symmetrical MS/WS | flat, frontal | 25–40 | locked-off, snap pans | even, bright | precise timing, cut on the reaction |
| Product desire | macro, CU | 3/4 hero, slightly low | 100 macro, 50 | slow slider, orbit, turntable | edge/rim light, gradients, specular kicks | slow-mo, smooth ramps |

## 2. Shot sizes

- **EWS / establishing**: geography, scale, isolation.
- **WS / full**: whole body + environment; action legibility; blocking.
- **MWS / cowboy**: mid-thigh up; groups, stand-offs.
- **MS**: waist up; conversation default; gesture.
- **MCU**: chest up; emotion with context.
- **CU**: face; emotion is the subject.
- **ECU**: eyes, mouth, detail; intensity, revelation.
- **Insert**: hands, objects, screens; information and rhythm.
- **OTS / two-shot / POV**: relationship, eyeline, subjectivity.

Coverage logic: establish → move in as emotion rises → pull out to release
or to reveal consequence.

## 3. Angle & height

Eye level = neutral. Low = power, threat, monumentality. High = vulnerability,
surveillance. Overhead/top-down = pattern, detachment, choreography. Dutch =
instability (overused; one is a choice, five is a habit). Ground level =
energy, kid/animal POV. Shoulder-height handheld = documentary presence.

## 4. Lenses (full-frame equivalents)

| Focal | Feel | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 14–20 | immersive, distorted edges | spaces, action from inside, comedy close-ups |
| 24–28 | wide but usable for people | environments with subject, walk-and-talks |
| 35 | the "honest" storyteller lens | handheld drama, medium shots |
| 50 | neutral, human-eye-ish | dialogue, everything |
| 85 | flattering portrait compression | close-ups, emotion |
| 135–200 | stacked, voyeuristic, background isolation | long-lens telephoto portraits, heat-haze, crowds |
| Macro | texture, scale shift | products, food, insects, ECUs |

Anamorphic: 2.39:1, oval bokeh, horizontal flares, breathing. Vintage:
soft, low-contrast, glowing. Tilt-shift: miniature effect or selective focal plane.
Depth of field: shallow isolates and romanticises; deep focus lets the audience
choose where to look and reads as "observed".

## 5. Camera movement

| Move | What it is | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Locked-off | tripod, no movement | composure, observation, comedy timing |
| Pan / tilt | rotate on axis | survey, follow, reveal (no parallax) |
| Dolly / push-in | camera travels toward subject | realisation, intimacy, threat |
| Pull-out | travels away | isolation, reveal of context, ending |
| Truck / tracking | lateral travel | parallel action, walk-and-talk, parallax depth |
| Pedestal | vertical travel, no tilt | reveal over an object, elevation |
| Crane / jib | sweeping vertical arc | grandeur, opening/closing statements |
| Orbit / arc | circle around subject | heroism, romance, disorientation if fast |
| Handheld | operator-held, shake | urgency, documentary truth, chaos |
| Gimbal / Steadicam | floating, stabilised | dreamlike following, long takes |
| Drone | aerial travel | scale, geography, transitions between worlds |
| Zoom (optical) | focal change, no travel | 70s / documentary / unease; snap zoom = comedy or shock |
| Dolly zoom (Vertigo) | push + zoom out (or reverse) | vertigo, sudden realisation |
| Whip pan | very fast pan with blur | energy, hidden transitions |
| FPV | drone flying through spaces | kinetic, spectacle |

Speed matters as much as type: a 2-second push says something different from a 12-second one.
Give speeds in prompts ("slow, ~10% closer over 5 s").

## 6. Composition

Rule of thirds; centred symmetry (control, formality, Wes-Anderson-style deadpan);
short-siding (looking toward the near frame edge) for unease; lead room for
movement; headroom (tight = pressure). Depth: foreground element + subject +
background makes 3D; "dirty" foreground (shoot past something) feels voyeuristic
and real. Frames within frames (doors, windows, mirrors) for entrapment or
observation. Negative space = isolation, minimalism, room for text.

## 7. Light recipes

- **Rembrandt**: key 45° side, 45° high; triangle of light on the shadow cheek. Classic drama.
- **Loop**: key slightly off-axis; small nose shadow loops toward the cheek. Flattering default.
- **Butterfly / Paramount**: key high and frontal; shadow under nose. Glamour, beauty.
- **Split**: key 90° side; half the face dark. Duality, menace.
- **Broad vs short**: lighting the side of the face toward camera (broad, open) vs away (short, sculpted, slimming, moodier).
- **Chiaroscuro / noir**: single hard source, ratio 8:1+, deep blacks, slatted shadows.
- **High-key**: bright, low ratio, white backgrounds. Comedy, commercial, clean product.
- **Low-key**: most of the frame dark, pools of light. Thriller, drama.
- **Motivated practicals**: lamps, screens, neon, candles in frame; set justifies the light.
- **Window light**: big soft directional key, natural falloff. Naturalism.
- **Backlight / rim**: separation, halo, heroism; with haze → volumetric beams.
- **Golden hour**: low warm sun, long shadows, flares. Nostalgia, romance.
- **Blue hour**: deep blue ambience + warm practicals. Melancholy, cinematic night-lite.
- **Mixed colour**: tungsten practicals vs cool moonlight/daylight. Night cinema staple.
- **Neon / cyberpunk**: saturated magenta/cyan, wet surfaces for reflections.
- **Top light**: interrogation, fatigue, eye sockets go dark.
- **Hard sun / high noon**: harsh, overexposed, heat; westerns, thrillers.

Describe light by **direction, quality, ratio, colour, motivation**. Example:
"hard key from high camera-right through blinds, 6:1, warm 3200K; cool fill
bouncing from a monitor; haze."

## 8. Colour & grade looks (in words a model understands)

- **Teal & orange**: skin warm, shadows/background pushed teal. Blockbuster default.
- **Bleach bypass**: desaturated, high contrast, silvery. War, grit.
- **Matte / faded film**: lifted blacks, soft highlights, muted. Indie, nostalgia.
- **Kodak-ish warm film**: warm mids, rich reds, gentle highlight roll-off, grain.
- **Fuji-ish**: greener shadows, cooler, delicate skin.
- **Day-for-night**: underexposed blue, contrast held.
- **Monochrome / B&W**: tonal contrast carries everything; specify grain and contrast.
- **Single accent colour**: desaturated world + one colour (a red coat).
- **Pastel / candy**: high-key, soft saturated pastels, low contrast.
- **Neon noir**: crushed blacks, saturated magenta/cyan, specular highlights.
- **Clean commercial**: neutral WB, bright, saturated but natural skin, no grain.

## 9. Continuity

- **180° rule**: keep the camera on one side of the action line; crossing it flips screen direction. Cross deliberately (on a move, or a neutral shot) or not at all.
- **Screen direction**: a journey left→right continues left→right.
- **Eyelines**: A looks camera-left to B, B looks camera-right to A.
- **30° rule**: cutting between two shots of the same subject needs ≥30° angle change or a size change, otherwise it's a jump cut (fine if intentional).
- **Light direction, time of day, weather, props, wardrobe, hair, wet/dry state**: track per scene.

## 10. Editing grammar

Hard cut · cut on action (hides the cut, keeps energy) · match cut (graphic, motion,
or sound rhyme) · jump cut (time compression, nervous energy, vlog grammar) · smash
cut (abrupt contrast) · J-cut / L-cut (sound leads/trails picture; smooths
dialogue, adds anticipation) · cross-cutting (parallel action, tension) · montage
(time compression, music-led) · cutaway / insert (information, rhythm, covering
edits) · whip / hidden transitions · dissolve (time passing, memory) · fade to
black (chapter end) · flash frames / strobe (impact, disorientation) · speed
ramp (slow on the key moment, back to speed) · freeze frame.

Rhythm principles: vary shot length to avoid metronome fatigue unless the
style is the metronome; cut when the information in a shot is spent; a long
hold after fast cutting lands like a punch; silence before the hit.

## 11. Pace benchmarks (rough, for orientation only)

Measure the reference instead of trusting these, but they help sanity-check plans.

| Form | Typical ASL |
|---|---|
| Slow cinema / long-take drama | 8–30 s+ |
| Contemporary feature drama | ~4–8 s |
| Contemporary action / trailer | ~1.5–3 s (trailers faster toward the end) |
| Music video | ~1–2.5 s, frequently on the beat |
| Commercial (15–30 s spot) | ~1–2.5 s |
| YouTube talking head with jump cuts | ~2–6 s |
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | ~1–3 s, hook in the first second |

Feature-film shot lengths have shortened markedly over the past century;
ASL is a style choice, not a quality score.
